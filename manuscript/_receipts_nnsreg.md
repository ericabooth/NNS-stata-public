# Receipts: nnsreg Stata Journal manuscript

Thesis (one sentence): nnsreg turns the established NNS partition into reportable segment ranges and slopes in Stata; its main value is interpretive output and a differently tuned comparison fit, while its predictive edge is limited to lower replication-to-replication dispersion in the clustered benchmark studied here.

## npregress series arm added (2026-07-05, after first QA pass)

Added `npregress series` as a comparison arm to the diagnostics table, Monte Carlo (table + box plot), and text, because it is the closest Stata-native competitor (data-driven-knot spline that posts estimation results) and the referee flagged its omission. It materially changed the honest framing:
- Smooth designs: kernel and series both beat NNS on average; series beats kernel 52% (water) / 62% (education) of draws, so the two npregress flavors are near-substitutes.
- Clustered design: series has the LOWEST mean true RMSE (0.467, ahead of NNS OLS 0.496/0.540) but the HIGHEST SD (0.333, > kernel 0.233, ~5x NNS OLS). So NNS OLS's clustered advantage is reliability, not raw average accuracy. Reframed abstract, 4.5, guidance, and conclusion accordingly. This strengthens the "do not overstate" goal: a strong Stata-native competitor exists, so nnsreg's distinct value rests on (1) segment-level interpretation neither npregress reports, and (2) stability on clustered support.
